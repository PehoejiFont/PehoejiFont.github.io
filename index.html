The problem in one picture

Here are the same five Taigi words rendered in five fonts through HarfBuzz (the text engine in Chrome, Firefox, Android, LibreOffice, and Linux): O͘-á, hō͘ (雨, rain), chia̍h (食, eat), thiⁿ (天, sky), and n̂g (黃, yellow).

Why POJ breaks, in three principles

  1. Unicode has no ready-made o͘ or 8th-tone letters. Computers store o͘ as o + combining dot above right (U+0358) and a̍ as a + combining vertical line above (U+030D). Together with superscript ⁿ (U+207F), most Latin fonts simply lack these characters. Unicode & Encoding →
  2. Mapping the codepoint is not enough. Fonts must position U+0358 at the top right of every toned o (including precomposed ó ò ô ō ŏ and capitals), swap i to dotless ı before U+030D, and run composition in ccmp (never optional liga). How Fonts Implement POJ →
  3. The same visible syllable arrives in multiple Unicode sequences. NFC (ó + U+0358), NFD (o + U+0301 + U+0358), and non-canonical input order (o + U+0358 + U+0301) must all shape identically across HarfBuzz, CoreText, and DirectWrite. Canonical Ordering →
