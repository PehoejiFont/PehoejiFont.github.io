Why does o͘ look wrong in my font?

A short guide to writing Taiwanese in Pe̍h-ōe-jī (白話字, POJ) on computers and phones: why some letters break, and how to pick a font that gets them right.

The problem in one picture

Here are the same five Taiwanese words in five fonts: O͘-á, hō͘ (雨, rain), chia̍h (食, eat), thiⁿ (天, sky) and n̂g (黃, yellow).

CorrectLesson One a free font with full POJ support
Dot in the wrong placeArial the dot of hō͘ lands in the space after the letter
Dot floats awayHelvetica the dot of capital O͘ sits too high
MissingEB Garamond empty boxes where the dot should be
Different styleGenYo Min (源樣明體) the dot is drawn tight, like a hook

The pictures show what Chrome, Firefox and Android draw. Macs can differ a little; see Mac and Windows.

Why it happens, in three sentences

  1. The computer has no ready-made o͘. It stores an o plus a separate “dot” sticker, and the font must glue them together. More →
  2. Many fonts never learned where the dot goes. They were made for English or European languages, which don't use it. More →
  3. The same letter can arrive in several different codes, and a good font has to handle all of them. More →
